Water mechanics for Earth's rivers. Hydrology was created primarily to provide water mechanics for the rivers generated by Earth and expand what players can do with them. It handles how water fills channels, flows between river levels and becomes rapids, including when players dig or build.

Earth supplies the geography: river courses, levels, slopes and discharge. Hydrology supplies the water behavior, with channel filling, smoother drops, constructible rapids and working mill races. Built for use with Earth; standalone gameplay outside Earth has not been tested for this release.

First public development release: 0.6.0. Core river mechanics are implemented, with further water features, testing and compatibility improvements planned.

Features

  • Channels that fill: supported channels at least two blocks wide fill with source water, fresh or salt, whether dug outward from the water or connected after digging. One-block-wide channels retain vanilla's six-block flowing-water reach.
  • Deep canals and basins: with Water flooding enabled, deeper excavations fill layer by layer from suitable feeding water. Uneven sea-canal beds can fill too; the additional uneven-bed rule also applies to freshwater canals outside natural river columns in compatible Earth worlds.
  • Smoother small drops: a one-block drop onto a lower pool forms a ramp of flowing water instead of a vertical face.
  • Build your own rapids: a surface fall of two or more blocks, including closely spaced smaller steps, creates rapid water. Vanilla water wheels can turn in these falls and mill races. Filling the drop back in lets ordinary water return outside Earth's designated rapids reaches.
  • Earth river integration: steep reaches remain rapid along their length and keep the river's flow direction in compatible Earth worlds. Turning water wheels can gain torque from the river's slope and discharge.
  • Waterfalls that settle: supported sources are protected from being drained by falling water, unsupported sources drain, and water in the air behaves as falling water after construction changes.

Installation

Built for Vintage Story 1.22. In singleplayer, install Hydrology in your Mods folder. In multiplayer, it is required on the server and on clients (Earth depends on it, so joining players download it automatically). On clients it also reduces excessive foam in wide rapids.

Use Hydrology with a compatible release of Earth. Hydrology uses vanilla water blocks, so removing it leaves no unknown mod blocks.

Water responds through normal liquid updates when nearby blocks change. Existing water is not periodically swept or regenerated; a previously stalled area may need a nearby block change to start updating.

Settings

In world settings, open Earth → Water flooding to control communicating vessels: deep canals and basins filling toward the level of their feeding water. This option is enabled by default in new worlds. Change it and reload the world or server for the new choice to apply. Existing worlds without this option retain their previous configuration behavior. It controls deep filling; other water rules keep their own settings.

Advanced settings are in ModConfig/hydrology.json, including filling, flattened drops, rapids, settling, river-wheel power and client foam. Administrators can use /hydrology stats for diagnostics.

Strongly recommended: under Survival challenges, enable Vintage Story's Prevent liquid source transport by buckets setting. Buckets then pour flowing water rather than creating new sources. Hydrology does not enable or enforce this setting; bucket source transport follows your vanilla world or server settings.

Compatibility and limits

Do not combine Hydrology with Realistic Water or Realistic Rapids. They change the same water-spreading behavior.

Only fresh water becomes rapids; vanilla has no salt-water rapid blocks. Deep filling follows local depth, support and containment rules. In compatible Earth worlds, natural river columns are excluded from communicating-vessel filling to preserve their stepped levels.

Managed ponds, dam failure, complete river drainage after diversion, and dedicated sluice or lock systems remain future development goals.
